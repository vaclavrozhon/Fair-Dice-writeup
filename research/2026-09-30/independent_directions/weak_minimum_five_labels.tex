\begin{theorem}
On five or six labels, the smallest support of a probability distribution
whose minimum is uniform on every triple is six. Arbitrary positive
weights on the supporting permutations are allowed.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The six-permutation family
\[
012345,\quad521340,\quad531204,\quad413250,\quad423105,\quad032145
\]
gives the upper bound on six labels, and deletion gives it on five.
Its triple-minimum counts are verified in
\path{verify_nine_dice_cloning.py}.
The weighted support theorem in
\path{weak_minimum_equality_obstruction.tex} gives the matching lower
bound on six labels, and excludes support at most four on five labels.
It remains to exclude a five-row distribution on five labels.

For every triple the five positive row weights are partitioned into
three classes of total weight $1/3$. At least one class is a singleton,
so at least one row has weight $1/3$. There can be at most two such
rows. If there are two, they are singleton classes for every triple;
the other three rows have the same minimum for every triple. Merge
these three rows into any one of them with their combined weight
$1/3$. This gives a forbidden three-row distribution on five labels.

Suppose there is exactly one row of weight $1/3$. In every triple,
the remaining four rows split into two pairs of weight $1/3$.
There are three perfect matchings on four rows. If at most two of
these matchings have both pair weights $1/3$, there is a fixed
two-element set of rows meeting every admissible pair once: for two
matchings take a bipartition of their four-cycle; for one matching
choose one endpoint of each pair. Together with the row of weight
$1/3$, these two rows have three different minima on every triple.
Giving the three selected rows equal weights creates another
forbidden three-row distribution.

All three matchings must therefore be admissible. Their pair-sum
equations force each of the four remaining row weights to be $1/6$.
Duplicating the weight-$1/3$ row gives six equally weighted rows,
two of which are identical. Relabel so that these two rows are
$12345$.

In every other row, the smallest label of any triple in the usual
order can never be its minimum: the two identical rows have already
used its entire quota. Consequently each other row starts with $4$
or $5$. The triple $\{3,4,5\}$ implies that exactly two rows start
with each of these labels.

Fix $a<b\le3$. In the triple $\{a,b,5\}$, the two rows starting with
$5$ use its quota, while the duplicated rows use $a$'s quota.
Thus both rows starting with $4$ must have $b<a$. Interchanging
$4,5$ shows that both rows starting with $5$ also have $b<a$.
All four rows therefore restrict to $321$ on $\{1,2,3\}$. The
minimum of this triple is $3$ four times and $2$ never, a contradiction.
\end{proof}
Together with the three-row construction on four labels, this gives
weighted minimum support values $3,3,6,6$ for $n=3,4,5,6$ respectively.
This concerns the minimum on triples only; it does not impose
pairwise-unbiased comparisons.
