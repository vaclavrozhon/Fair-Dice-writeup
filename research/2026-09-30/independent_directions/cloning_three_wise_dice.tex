\begin{theorem}\label{clone}
Let $W$ be a three-wise fair word on $n$ letters, each occurring $M$
times, and distinguish a letter $X$. For a face of any other letter
$D$, let $h$ count occurrences of $X$ before that face. Suppose
\begin{equation}\label{square}
 \sum_{\text{faces of }D}h^2=M^3/3\qquad(D\ne X).
\end{equation}
Let $\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_M$ be permutations of $k$ new labels such
that in every triple of labels, each label is earliest in exactly
$M/3$ of the permutations. Replace occurrence $r$ of $X$ by the
palindrome $\rho_r\rho_r^{\rm rev}$, and replace every occurrence
of every other letter by two consecutive copies. The resulting word
is three-wise fair on $n-1+k$ letters, each occurring $2M$ times.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Call the $k$ new labels clones. Each replacement palindrome contains
two occurrences of every clone and is pairwise fair: both orders of
any two clones have two scattered occurrences. A concatenation of
$h$ such blocks is again pairwise fair, and each order of two clones
occurs $2h^2$ times.

A triple containing at most one clone inherits its order counts from
the corresponding triple of $W$, multiplied by $2^3$. Now consider
two clones $A,B$ and one old letter $D$. Pairwise fairness of $W$
gives $\sum h=M^2/2$, while \eqref{square} gives $\sum h^2=M^3/3$.
For each original face of $D$ there are two copies in the new word.
The number of occurrences of the order $ABD$ is therefore
\[
 4\sum h^2=4M^3/3=(2M)^3/6.
\]
The same holds for $BAD$. The two orders with $D$ first follow from
$\sum(M-h)^2=M^3/3$. Each order with $D$ in the middle has count
\[
 8\sum h(M-h)=8(M^3/2-M^3/3)=4M^3/3.
\]

It remains to check three clones. In a single block
$\rho\rho^{\rm rev}$, a prescribed order $ABC$ occurs zero times
if $B$ is earliest among its three labels in $\rho$, and twice
otherwise. The within-block contribution over all $M$ blocks is
therefore $4M/3$. Occurrences using three distinct blocks contribute
$8\binom M3$ to each prescribed order. Occurrences using exactly
two blocks contribute $8\binom M2$: the doubled block may contain
the first two or last two letters, each contributing $2\cdot2=4$.
Thus every order has total count
\[
 8\binom M3+8\binom M2+4M/3=4M^3/3,
\]
as required.
\end{proof}

Apply this with $M=6$, distinguished label $X=1$, and the four-letter
seed
\[
 W=012332103102201321033012.
\]
The seed is three-wise fair. Its prefix-$1$ counts at the faces of
the other letters are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
D& (h_1,\ldots,h_6)&\sum h_i&\sum h_i^2\\\hline
0&(0,2,3,3,5,5)&18&72\\
2&(1,1,3,3,4,6)&18&72\\
3&(1,1,2,4,5,5)&18&72.
\end{array}
\]
Use the following six permutations on six new labels:
\[
 012345,\quad521340,\quad531204,\quad413250,\quad423105,\quad032145.
\]
For each of the twenty triples, each member is earliest in exactly
two permutations. After cloning, relabel the three surviving old
labels $0,2,3$ as $6,7,8$. The resulting word is exactly the certificate
\path{../partial_fairness/nine_dice_twelve_three_wise.json}.

\begin{corollary}
The minimum common face count for three-wise fair dice is
$M_3(n)=12$ for every $5\le n\le9$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The construction gives nine dice with twelve faces; delete labels for
the smaller values of $n$. The independent six-face enumeration and
mod-four extension obstruction in
\path{six_face_extension_congruence.tex} rule out five six-face dice,
and hence rule them out for all $n\ge5$. Three-wise fairness requires
$6\mid M^3$, equivalently $6\mid M$. Therefore no smaller positive
common face count is possible.
\end{proof}
