ANY FIXED NUMBER OF POLYNOMIAL-SIZE FAIR DICE
30 September 2026. Complete proof; three independent mathematical reviews.

Main theorem
  independent_directions/fixed_number_polynomial_dice.tex (+PDF)
For each fixed k, every sufficiently large n admits a fully permutation-fair
finite family in which k specified equiprobable dice have the SAME face
count at most C_k*n^{c_k}. All labels are distinct. The remaining n-k dice
may have one common finite count, with no bound asserted here. A subsequently
reviewed uniform complexity audit gives the stronger quantitative count
  S <= (n+2^k+2)^{exp(C*k^2)}
for all n>k, with absolute C; see step 7 below. Neither statement is a
literature priority claim or formal proof-assistant verification.

5. Global induction:
   independent_directions/fixed_number_polynomial_dice.tex
   Begin with one uniform pure density. Use reserve
       q_j = 2^{k-j+1}-1.
   On a cell of mass gamma and uniform-grid coordinate t=Qx-A, prescribe
   K=N*gamma with N divisible by the old common moment denominator.
   Then K times every required normalized input moment is an integer.
   Apply step 4 simultaneously to all cells and step 3 to reset the grid.
   In any order integral the new variable shares an old pure cell with
   at most one existing special variable, so local two-variable ordered
   moments preserve the whole global order law.

6. Fully finite realization:
   size_bounds/clustered_multi_exceptional_lifting.tex
   Atomize each final rational pure cell using the every-count separated
   quadrature from step 2, with one common polynomial mass denominator.
   This gives equal polynomial specified counts, distinct interior nodes,
   at least (n-k)^2 nodes per cluster, and rational cluster moments.
   The multi-boundary lifting theorem then discretizes all remaining
   uniforms without changing the k specified face counts.

7. Uniform dependence on k (additional quantitative audit):
   size_bounds/uniform_prefix_conditioning.tex
   size_bounds/uniform_fixed_number_complexity.tex
   They bound each insertion/reset complexity increase by P^{(q+2)^C}.
   The initial uniform raw-moment denominator costs base^{O(2^k)},
   and the reserve recurrence yields the final exponent exp(C*k^2).
   Consequently c*sqrt(log n) specified dice can have equal counts at
   most exp(n^{1/2}); k=o(sqrt(log n)) gives exp(n^{o(1)}) counts.
   These are subexponential counts, not polynomial counts for growing k.
   size_bounds/FIXED_K_PROOF_GUIDE.txt has the latest expanded dependency map.

What remains open here
- Making two or more specified dice simultaneously O(n^2) faces.
- Improving the proved uniform count bound as k grows with n, especially
  retaining polynomial-in-n counts for an unbounded number of dice.
- In particular, logarithmically many dice of <=n^C faces for absolute C;
  the root note many_small_dice_reduction.tex explains why that would
  imply an exponential-total construction for the original Q1.
- The article's total-size and polynomial-equalization questions.
