                          RATIONAL DESIGNS

               ZHEN CUI, JIACHENG XIA, AND ZIQING XIANG


       Abstract. The existence of designs over R on a path-connected space
       has been proved by Seymour and Zaslavsky. This paper studies for the
       first time rational designs, that is designs over Q. We establish the
       existence of rational designs on an open connected space under certain
       necessary conditions. Consequently, we show that there exist rational
       designs on rational simplicial complexes and spherical designs over some
       real abelian extension of Q.

                               1. Introduction
  Designs are finite subsets of a given space that approximate the whole
space nicely. In this paper, we focus on geometric designs, which are also
known as cubature formula [Möl79; GS81] and averaging sets [SZ84; Wag91].
They were first introduced by Delsarte, Goethals and Seidel in [DGS77] for
spheres, and later studied for other spaces.

equipped with the spherical measure ν d . A spherical design of strength t, or
simply a spherical t-design, on S d is a nonempty finite set X ⊆ S d such that

   The current known methods cannot construct rational spherical designs
of high strengths [BB09]. Shells of lattices, such as the ones in Example 1.2,
are only known to be spherical designs of strength at most 11, and Exam-
ple 1.2(ii) yields a spherical 12-design if and only if some generalization of
Lehmer’s conjecture does not hold. On S d with d ≥ 2, orbits of a point
under the action of finite reflection groups can only yield spherical designs
of strength at most 191, and they are rarely rational.
   Most proofs on the existence of spherical designs rely on the completeness
of the field R. As Q is not complete in R, they cannot be adapted to prove
the existence of rational spherical designs. Perturbing real spherical designs
does not work either, since it will inevitably introduce weights of points in
the designs, which are not allowed in the definition of spherical designs. In
fact, the existence of rational spherical 12-designs is still unknown.
   It is then natural to ask: over what small fields, there exist spherical
designs on S d for all strengths. Theorem 1.3 proves the existence of spherical
designs for all strengths over some real abelian extension of Q, which also
provides a new proof of the existence of spherical designs over R. The proof
of Theorem 1.3, presented in § 7.2, employs results from analytic number
theory, approximation theory and convex geometry, which let us overcome
major difficulties arising from the incompleteness of proper subfields of R.

1.2. Rational geometric designs. In this section, we consider designs on
general spaces Z. We call a topological space equipped with a good measure
a levelling space (see Definition 2.1), and define (unweighted) designs and
weighted designs on levelling spaces in Definition 3.1. This definition was
essentially given by Seymour and Zaslavsky [SZ84], who also proved that
designs on Z over R always exist if the space Z is path-connected. There
is a large body of literature on designs on general spaces, such as: complex
spherical designs [RS14], Euclidean designs [NS88; DS89], designs on pro-
jective spaces [Hog82], designs on Grassmannians [BCN02; BBC04], designs
on (projective) unitary groups [Sco08; RS09], and designs on polynomial
spaces [God88; BBD99].
   In many talks, Bannai proposed the question of the existence of rational
t-designs on a given space Z for all natural number t. The similar question
for weighted rational designs has a simple answer: we prove in § 3 that
Condition 3.2 on the space Z is a necessary and sufficient condition for the
existence of weighted rational designs. However, (unweighted) rational de-
signs are much more difficult to find, and we aim to provide first information
on their existence. In particular, we prove the following main result on open
connected spaces.
Theorem 1.4. Let Z be a levelling space such that Z is a topological sub-
space of Rd with d ≥ 1, and assume that Z satisfies Condition 3.2. Let t
be a natural number. If Z is open connected, then there exists a natural
number n0 such that for every natural number n ≥ n0 , there exist rational
t-designs on Z of size n.
  Theorem 1.4 follows from the stronger Theorem 7.1, where we prove the
existence of rational designs on algebraically path-connected spaces (see § 6).
Theorems 1.4 and 7.1 are proved in § 7.2 and § 7.1, respectively.
  As a corollary of Theorem 1.4, we show the existence of rational designs
on rational simplicial complexes in Theorem 1.5, which is proved in § 7.3.
Theorem 1.5. Let Z be a simplicial k-complex in Rd for some positive
integer k, equipped with the k-dimensional Hausdorff measure. Suppose that
4                ZHEN CUI, JIACHENG XIA, AND ZIQING XIANG

the vertices of Z are rational points. Then, there exist rational t-designs on
Z for all natural numbers t.
  Another corollary of Theorem 1.4 is the existence of rational interval t-
designs. We provide a more direct proof in Corollary 5.4.

If we parametrize rational points on S 1 , then we would get a Diophantine
equation whose degree is larger than the number of variables, and the au-
thors could not apply classical circle method in analytic number theory to
solve it.
   Under the necessary conditions, path-connectedness and algebraically path-
connectedness of the space Z guarantee the existence of designs on Z over
reals and over rationals, respectively. There are many spaces Z which are
not path-connected, yet we can still find designs on Z over reals. Euclidean
designs are such examples. It is an interesting problem to find a sufficient
condition of some other type for the existence of designs, which may shed
some light on the existence of rational spherical designs.
   One referee asks if it is possible to find spherical t-designs X on S d such
that the inner products between points in X are all rational. It turns out that
finding such spherical designs is tantamount to finding rational designs on
some ellipsoid. Similar to rational designs on spheres, Theorem 1.4 cannot
be applied to find rational designs on ellipsoids.

1.4. Structure of the paper. The paper is organized as follows. In § 2,
we introduce the concept of levelling space, which provides a framework to
talk about designs in a general setup. We then discuss properties of de-
signs and construct weighted designs using convex geometry. In § 3, we
prove that Condition 3.2 is a necessary and sufficient condition of the ex-
istence of weighted rational designs. In § 4, we analyze the possible total
measures of integer-weighted designs. § 5 provides the analytic number the-
oretic arguments which we need in § 7. § 6 is concerned with algebraic
path-connectivity and proves that open connected spaces are algebraically
path-connected spaces using approximation theory. Finally, in § 7, we prove
Theorem 7.1, from which the main result Theorem 1.4 follows, together with
its corollaries, including Theorems 1.3 and 1.5.

                     2. Levelling spaces and designs
   In this section, we discuss certain topological measure spaces which we
call levelling spaces. Some basic properties and constructions of levelling
spaces are given in § 2.1. In § 2.2, we first define an equivalence relation of
levelling spaces, and then define designs on a levelling space. In § 2.3, we
apply some results from convex geometry to construct weighted designs on
arbitrary levelling spaces.
2.1. Levelling spaces and their properties. A strictly positive measure
space X = (X, µX ) is a Hausdorff topological space X equipped with a
measure µX such that every nonempty open set has positive measure. In
particular, for a discrete space this means that all points have positive mea-
sures. The set X is said to be the support of X .
Definition 2.1. A levelling space X is a nonempty strictly positive measure
space of finite total measure | X |. It is called finite if its size # X is finite.
   Throughout this paper, a levelling space X is always written in calligraphy
font X in order to distinguish the levelling space X from its support, the
topological space X. For two levelling spaces X and Z, by saying X is a
subspace of Z, denoted by X ⊆ Z, we only mean that the support X of X
is a topological subspace of the support Z of Z. In particular, we do not
assume any relation between the measures µX and µZ . Similarly, we may
write X ⊆ Z or X ⊆ Z to mean that X is a topological subspace of Z.
Definition 2.2. Let k ⊆ R be a subset. A levelling space X = (X, µX ) is
k-weighted if the image of µX is in k.
   Typically, we take k to be either Z or Q, and call the levelling space
integer-weighted or rational-weighted, respectively. Recall that, as a mea-
sure space, the image of µX is always nonnegative. A finite levelling space
X is just a nonempty finite weighted set (X, ωX ) where ωX : X → R> 0 .

In particular, a finite integer-valued levelling space may be viewed as a
nonempty finite multi-set.
  Now, we introduce some operations on levelling spaces.
Definition 2.3. Let X = (X, µX ), and Y = (Y, µY ) be two levelling spaces
whose subtopologies on the set intersection X ∩ Y agree. The sum of X and
Y, denoted by X + Y, is defined to be (X ∪ Y, µX + µY ), where X ∪ Y is the
topological union of X and Y , and µX + µY is the sum of the measures µX
and µY . More precisely, a subset E ⊆ X ∪ Y is measurable in X + Y if and
only if E ∩ X and E ∩ Y are measurable in X and Y, respectively, and the
measure is defined via (µX + µY )(E) := µX (E ∩ X) + µY (E ∩ Y ).
Definition 2.4. Let X = (X, µX ) be a levelling space, and let c be a positive
real number. We set c X := (X, c µX ), where c µX is a constant scalar of
µX .
Definition 2.5. Let X and Y be topological spaces and f : X → Y a
continuous map. For a levelling space W = (W, µW ) with W ⊆ X, the
image of W under f is defined to be f (W) := (f (W ), f∗ µW ), where f (W ) is
the image of W under f as a topological space, and f∗ µW is the pushforward
of the measure µW . That is to say, a subset E ⊆ f (W ) is measurable in
f (W) if and only if f −1 (E) ∩ W is measurable in W, and (f∗ µW )(E) :=
µW (f −1 (E) ∩ W ).

                       6. Algebraic path-connectivity
  Algebraically path-connected space is a variant of ordinary path-connected
where “algebraic paths” are used instead of ordinary paths.
                                 RATIONAL DESIGNS                              21

Definition 6.1. Let Z ⊆ Rd be a subset and F ⊆ R a subfield. The
set Z is called F-algebraically path-connected provided that for every finite
subset X ⊆ Z ∩ Fd , there exists a polynomial map p ∈ F[x]d such that
X ⊆ p((0, 1)F ) ⊆ Z ∩ Fd .
   In this section, we give a sufficient condition of F-algebraic path-connectivity
in Theorem 6.2 below.
Theorem 6.2. Let Z ⊆ Rd be an open connected subset. For every subfield
F ⊆ R, Z is F-algebraically path-connected.
   In § 6.1, we use approximation theory to prove Theorem 6.2, which is
used in the proof of Theorem 7.1 to construct a good path in an open
connected subset of Rd . In § 6.2, we show in Proposition 6.5 that the real
unit sphere is not F-algebraically path-connected for any subfield F ⊆ R.
In Proposition 6.8, we show that the real unit sphere does not satisfy a
property weaker than algebraic path-connectivity either. Note that if this
weaker property were obtained for the real unit sphere, we could obtain from
it the existence of rational spherical designs. Propositions 6.5 and 6.8 are
not used in the remainder of the article.
