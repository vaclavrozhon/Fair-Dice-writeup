\begin{proposition}
The fifteen centered five-row, four-column comparison moment
equations have a solution with all coordinates in $\mathbb Z_5$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For nonempty $S\subseteq\{1,2,3,4\}$ use the integer polynomials
\[
 F_S(x)=3\sum_{i=1}^5\prod_{j\in S}x_{j,i}-b_S,
 \quad b_S=\begin{cases}5,&|S|=2,\\3,&|S|=4,\\0,&|S|\text{ odd}.\end{cases}
\]
Take the initial four columns
\[
 (22,15,33,31,24),\quad (97,65,58,31,124),
 \quad(14,6,88,0,17),\quad(62,75,113,106,19).
\]
In subset order by size and then lexicographically their residuals are
\[
\begin{split}
(375,1125,375,1125,26875,11125,29875,26875,69250,34750,\\
764250,1740375,1085250,2190750,66855375).
\end{split}
\]
All residuals are divisible by $125$. Flattening the variables
column by column with indices $0,\ldots,19$, choose the fifteen
Jacobian columns
\[
0,1,2,3,5,6,7,10,11,12,15,16,17,4,8.
\]
The resulting square matrix $J$ has determinant
\[
 -254169067184538400674529220966400,
\]
of 5-adic valuation two, and has rank thirteen modulo five. Hence
every cofactor is divisible by five, so all entries of $J^{-1}$ lie
in $5^{-1}\mathbb Z_5$.

Fixing the other five variables, the map $y\mapsto y-J^{-1}F(y)$
preserves the ball $x+25\mathbb Z_5^{15}$ and is a strict contraction
there. Indeed its initial displacement has valuation at least two,
and the change of the polynomial Jacobian throughout the ball has
entries divisible by $25$. The contraction principle supplies an
exact zero of all the moment polynomials.

The integer certificate is stored in
\texttt{general\_q5\_hensel\_certificate.json}; the script
\texttt{lift\_general\_q5.py} reproduces the residual, determinant,
and inverse valuation checks by exact rational arithmetic.
\end{proof}
This is a statement over $\mathbb Q_5$, with no assertion of a
rational or real monotone point. Together with the separate 2-adic
and 3-adic certificates, it rules out local insolubility at the three
smallest primes as an obstruction to five-row local models.
