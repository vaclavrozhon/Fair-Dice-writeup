Quadratic exceptional-die theorem: short proof guide
30 September 2026

STATEMENT
Let g(n) be the fewest faces that one specified die can have in a
permutation-fair family of n finite equiprobable dice, allowing the other
dice to have any finite sizes. Then g(n)=Theta(n^2).
The lower bound is universal. The upper construction may use extremely
large other dice, so this does not solve minimum total size or polynomial
equalization. The manuscript's exponential-per-die conjecture is false.
In fact the lower bound only needs the distinguished die to win each
subset containing it with probability reciprocal to the subset size;
the full distribution of complete orders need not be uniform.

LOWER-BOUND DEPENDENCIES, IN LOGICAL ORDER
Paths below are relative to research/2026-09-30/.

1. rational_designs/asymptotic/finite_endpoint_odds_balance.tex
   Exact mixed moments determine every success/failure-pattern mass.
   Monotonicity makes large odds a prefix. Conditioning on a suitable
   number of failures yields max(1-p_i) <= C(s+1)/m and endpoint row-mass
   bound m Pr(s<=t) <= C(t+1), where s=sum_i(1-p_i).

2. rational_designs/asymptotic/weighted_success_subset_moments.tex
   A finite elementary-symmetric calculation controls the bias and
   variance when an ell-subset is sampled with probability proportional
   to the product of its success probabilities. Newton's inequality
   gives the needed nonpositive pairwise inclusion covariances.

3. power_per_die_lower_bound.tex
   Only its stable bilinear Laguerre comparison is needed downstream:
   degree d<=sqrt(m), error C B_1 B_2 d/m. It follows from exact
   polarization, centered variance, the Gaussian off-diagonal Laguerre
   bound, and items 1-2. It also proves the earlier Omega(n^(3/2)) bound.
   The Gaussian facts are separately derived in
   size_bounds/gaussian_laguerre_variance_lemmas.tex.

4a. rational_designs/asymptotic/quantitative_relative_endpoint_balance.tex
    Marked and unmarked versions of item 3, tested against geometrically
    weighted Laguerre sums, prove the stronger rowwise bound
      max_i(1-p_i) <= C(s+m^(-1/3))/m.

4b. size_bounds/elementary_endpoint_cdf_bootstrap.tex
    Item 3 first gives F_nu(t)<=C(t+m^(-1/2)), where nu=m·law(alpha s).
    Applying the off-diagonal Gaussian bound directly to the tapered
    Laguerre derivative gives a uniform all-orders estimate. The same
    calculation for normalized squares through degree m^(3/4) improves
    this to F_nu(t)<=C(t+m^(-3/4)).
    This step uses neither item 4a nor the final quadratic theorem.

5. size_bounds/quadratic_per_die_lower_bound.tex
   Combine 4a and 4b. The all-orders Gaussian estimate permits degree
   d=kappa m with a fixed small absolute kappa. The signed taper forces
   the final row to alpha s=O(1/m); a normalized square then bounds its
   tilted atom by O(1/m), and its original weight by O(1/m^2).
   Reverse/complement for the first row. Uniform die weights are 1/K,
   so K=Omega(m^2), with m=n-1.

UPPER-BOUND DEPENDENCIES
  size_bounds/polynomial_exceptional_die.tex
  size_bounds/centered_bump_simplification.tex  (optional shorter variant)
Start with a real equal-weight degree-O(n) interval rule of O(n^2) nodes.
The classical existence input is Gilboa-Peled, Theorem 1.1,
https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.01505 (uniform-weight specialization).
Regularize the real rule, approximate nodes rationally, and repair every
order integral by disjoint rational step-density perturbations of the
other dice. Full zero moments kill mixed perturbation terms. Replace each
rational constant-density interval by an already existing finite fair
block on the other n-1 labels. This yields a finite uniform distinguished
die of O(n^2) faces; all other dice can be made equally sided.

REVIEW AND SCOPE
The full quadratic proof and its upper construction each passed three
independent mathematical reviews. The elementary dependency simplification
also passed independent full reviews. These are not machine formal proofs.
The final lower-bound chain needs no external Laguerre asymptotic theorem:
all analytic inequalities are derived from elementary Gaussian integrals,
Laguerre polynomial identities, and finite subset algebra.
