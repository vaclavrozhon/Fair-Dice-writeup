Variable kernel:
- Normalization ell(sin(theta_a)+1/nu) gives b_a comparable to
  sqrt(a/m)+1/m, uniformly including the endpoint. The beta integral
  has fixed positive lower/upper bounds and the reflected angular term
  obeys the stated positive real envelope.
- On the relative annulus, Cauchy radius min(c*a,k*b_a/C) yields both
  derivative terms. The capped centered series is no larger than the
  uncapped comparison scale because m*a is bounded below.
- Sharp row balance makes the population range O(a). Success-weighted
  subset means have subGaussian variance O(H^2/m), hence leaving the
  relative annulus costs exp(-c*m), uniformly down to a=A_0/m.
- The far-angle Cauchy estimate retains the real angular envelope with
  an exponential-in-k loss; no k<<sqrt(m*a) restriction is needed.
  When its radius crosses an endpoint, the square-root inverse-angle
  estimate is still <=C*k/d; it is not necessary to linearize there.
- Sharp variance cancels the powers of v in the derivative denominator.
  Choosing d/m small leaves the success-weight exponential reserve.
- Integrating against nu([0,t])<=C(t+1/m) gives exactly the unmarked
  (m*a)^(1/2-r) tail and the marked extra factor a. At v=0 only the
  already controlled scalar term remains. The high-v tail is covered
  by the prior global Bernstein-Walsh estimate.

Assembly:
- Deleted-coordinate sharp balance and CDF hold with absolute constants.
- The local-mass supremum is finite for each m. Its covers use V+C_T;
  the bottom boundary comes from sharp CDF and the top from the already
  proved fixed-annulus mass theorem. Small fixed a_1 absorbs V.
- After obtaining a finite upper bound, increasing T and decreasing a_0
  yields the positive kernel-mass lower bound. No uniformity of the
  fixed-annulus constants as its endpoint tends to zero is assumed.
- The normalized maximum |h_i^*|/(sqrt(y/m)+1/m) handles both boundary
  regimes: sharp balance for bounded m*y and the interior theorem for
  fixed positive y. Large maxima are confined to 2T/m<=y<=a_2.
- Maximality controls all columns on the relative neighborhood. The
  deleted-coordinate displacement is o(b(y)), uniformly on bounded y.
- The favorable window is strictly to one side, avoiding flat-row
  ambiguity. Large normalized deviation keeps it inside the positive
  relative annulus, without changing the earlier fixed T.
- Positive window mass defeats local tails; the far marked tail divided
  by Delta is O((m*a)^(1-r)/D). The comparison error divided by Delta
  is O(sqrt(a/m)+a), so choosing a_2 small and then m large closes the
  contradiction. All choices have a consistent order.

Conclusion supported: for every fixed L, uniformly for 0<=s<=L,
  max_i |m(1-p_i)-s| <= C_L (sqrt(s/m)+1/m).
This is a mathematical review, not formal verification.

Additional full review: intermediate_endpoint_sharpness.tex
PASS. The degree-m Gauss rule uses Q=ceil((m+1)/2); choosing
j=ceil(sqrt(m*lambda)) uniformly for 1/m<=lambda<=L gives
1-a comparable to lambda/m and weight v>=c sqrt(lambda)m^(-3/2).
The zero-angle estimate was independently checked at
https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.16.E2 . The Bernstein inequality was checked
at https://dlmf.nist.gov/18.14.E3 with alpha=beta=0, equivalently
18.14.E7 with Gegenbauer parameter1/2. Combined with the exact
Christoffel-kernel identity, these give a uniform weight bound; no
fixed-index asymptotic is extrapolated to growing j.
The one-sided CDF jump provides discrepancy >=3v/8 at a continuity
point t. Exactness of x^m gives the Gaussian tail <=t^(-m)/(m+1),
so total deficit is comparable to lambda. The finite realization
perturbation preserves both comparability and the lower deviation.
The theorem correctly permits a separate family for each m,lambda,
and prescribes s only up to fixed factors. No face-count bound is given.
