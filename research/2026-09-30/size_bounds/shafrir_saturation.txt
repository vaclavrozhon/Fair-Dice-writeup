                                                                                                    Abstract
                                                     If M is a submonoid of a f.g. nilpotent group G, and M G′ is a finite index subgroup of G, then M
                                                 itself is a finite index subgroup of G. If M G′ = G, then M = G. This generalizes a well-known theorem
                                                 for subgroups of f.g. nilpotent groups. As a result, we give an algorithm for the identity problem in
                                                 nilpotent groups.


                                           1     Introduction
                                           Given upper unitriangular matrices B1 , ..., Bn ∈ U Tn (Z), is there a product of the Bi ’s (allowing for repe-
                                           titions) that equals the identity matrix In ? In this work we present an efficient algorithm for this problem.
                                           More generally, the identity problem for a f.g. group G is the following decision problem: Given a finite subset
                                           A ⊂ G, is eG ∈ A+ ? The elements of A are given as words in a fixed generating set X of G. We show an algo-
                                           rithm for this problem when G is nilpotent. The matrix version is a special case, since any A ∈ U Tn (Z) can
                                           be easily expressed as a word in the generating set of the elementary matrices X = {eij | i < j} ⊂ U Tn (Z)1

                                           1.1     A saturation theorem
                                           It is well-known that if G is a f.g. nilpotent group, and H ≤ G is a subgroup such that HG′ is a finite index
                                           subgroup of G, then in fact H itself is a finite index subgroup of G. Also, if HG′ = G, then H = G. We
                                           show that the same holds even if H is a submonoid and not assumed to be closed under inverses a-priori.

                                           1.2     Related results
                                           Bell and Potapov [BP10] showed that the Identity problem is undecidable for SL4 (Z). Ko, Niskanen, and
                                           Potapov [KNP18] showed it’s decidable for the Heisenberg groups Hn (Q). Dong [Don22] proved decidability
                                           for U T4 (Q), and extended it to nilpotent groups of class at most 10 [Don24], with assistance of computer
                                           algebra software. The Identity Problem is contained in the Submonoid Memebership Problem, which Ro-
                                           mankov showed to be undecidable for nilpotent groups of class 2 [Rom22]. While preparing this paper,
                                           a similar saturation theorem was announced in an arXiv preprint by Bodart [Bod24, Proposition 2.5], to
                                           appear in a future joint paper by Bodart, Ciobanu, and Metcalf2 .

Theorem 1. Let G be a finitely generated nilpotent group, H ≤ G a subgroup such that [G : HG′ ] < ∞.

    We can now state our main theorem:
Theorem 2. Assume G is a finitely generated nilpotent group and M ≤ G a submonoid of G. If M G′ is a
finite index subgroup of G, so is M itself.
Proof. We use induction on the nilpotency class c of G. For c = 1 there is nothing to prove. As-
sume c > 1. Define Z = γc−1 (G). By induction, M Z is a finite index subgroup of G. We choose
z1 , z2 , . . . , zn ∈ Z ∩ {[x, y] | x ∈ G, y ∈ γc−2 (G)} generating Z. Note that we make sure all zi are com-
mutators for Proposition 3 to be applicable. Set

Corollary 1. Let G be f.g. and nilpotent, M ≤ G a submonoid, and M G′ = G. Then M = G.
Proof. By Theorem 2, M is a subgroup of G. Now we are in the classical case, see Theorem 1 or [CMZ17,
Theorem 7.18].

   Now we present the algorithm. We actually solve a more general problem than the identity problem:
Given A ⊂ G, find Ainv = {g ∈ A | g −1 ∈ A∗ }. Equivalently, Ainv is the set of a ∈ A which are part of a

algorithm, and then prove its correctness.
