LITERATURE AUDIT: REDUCED SIGNATURES AND POSITIVE SATURATION
30 September 2026. This records verified connections, not a proof of novelty.

1. Reduced free group / integral Lie coordinates.
Jacques Darne, Milnor invariants of braids and welded braids up to homotopy,
Algebraic & Geometric Topology 24 (2024), 1277--1320.
https://doi.org/10.2140/agt.2024.24.1277
https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.10677
Primary full text saved as darne_reduced_free_group.pdf and .txt.
Definition 1.4 gives the reduced associative algebra (words with repeated
letters vanish). Proposition 1.9 gives multilinear ranks, Theorem 1.12
identifies the reduced Lie algebra, and Corollary 1.13 gives the reduced
Magnus embedding. Lemma 6.15 is exactly the fixed-last-letter integral
right-nested bracket basis used in signed_fair_signatures.tex.
The underlying reduced free group is classical, going back to Milnor.
Thus neither that algebra/group nor the integral bracket basis is new.
The dice-specific arithmetic characterization of the fair ray is an
application of these tools; no earlier source for that exact consequence
has been located in this audit.

2. Positive word equivalence.
Wen Chean Teh, On strong M-equivalence, International Journal of
Foundations of Computer Science 27 (2016), 545--556.
https://doi.org/10.1142/S0129054116500155
https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.07354
Primary full text saved as teh_strong_m_equivalence.pdf and .txt.
Theorem 3.3 identifies strong M-equivalence with equality of all scattered
subword counts having no repeated letters. This is exactly equality of
our reduced signatures on positive words. Proposition 3.2 gives the
cancellative congruence property. For equal letter counts, permutation
fairness is equivalent to invariance of the strong M-equivalence class
under all letter relabelings.
Searches for symmetric/permutation-invariant strong M-equivalence and
related Parikh matrix terminology did not locate a previously stated
minimum fair-word-size or eventual primorial saturation result. This is
only a search outcome, not an assertion that none exists.

3. Prefix completion.
The manuscript's existing all-n!-relabelings symmetrization lemma already
implies universal equal-count fair prefix completion: balance the initial
counts and put the identity relabeling first at every symmetrization.
Consequently that equal-count completion lemma should be attributed to
existing symmetrization, rather than advertised as a new theorem.
The weighted-conjugation extension to any prescribed positive rational
count ray is developed in eventual_arbitrary_profiles.tex.

4. Ore and positive nilpotency identities.
R. I. Grigorchuk, On growth in group theory, Proceedings of ICM Kyoto
1990, vol. I, printed pp. 325--338, especially Sections 5.1--5.3, p.331.
https://www.mathunion.org/fileadmin/ICM/Proceedings/ICM1990.1/ICM1990.1.ocr.pdf
Full primary volume was downloaded and relevant pages extracted into
grigorchuk_icm1990.txt. Section 5.1 attributes the positive nilpotency
identity to A. I. Malcev, Nilpotent semigroups (1953). With intervening
variables set to the identity, it gives A_0=a, B_0=b,
A_{j+1}=A_j B_j, B_{j+1}=B_j A_j, hence A_n=B_n in a class-n nilpotent
group. This supplies the 2^n-block common right multiple in the final
quantitative_eventual_sizes.tex. It is a classical identity.
Section 5.2 also states that cancellative semigroups of subexponential
growth satisfy Ore. The older growth-collision argument was independently
rediscovered here and has been replaced by the sharper Malcev identity.
The related primary 1988 paper is Cancellative semigroups of power growth,
Math. Notes 43(3), 175--183, https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01138837;
its metadata was verified, but its full text was not successfully read.

5. What the present synthesis proves.
Combining signed fair-ray integrality, positive fraction clearing, and
fair completion yields eventual realizability of every arithmetically
admissible multiple on any fixed positive rational count ray. The
primitive equal ray has period primorial(n). The current quantitative
conductor upper bound is exp(O(n^2 log n)); it does not improve the
smallest-family factorial-size upper bound. These exact consequences
have been proved and independently reviewed in the research notes, but
their historical novelty is not established by this limited audit.

2026 rational quadrature literature update (checked 30 September 2026)
Mishima, Lu, Sawa, Uchida, Geometric designs and Hilbert-Kamke equations
of degree five for classical orthogonal polynomials, Research in Number
Theory 12, article39 (published31 March2026), DOI10.1007/s40993-026-00723-4.
Primary full HTML: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40993-026-00723-4
The main small-node classifications concern the arcsine/Chebyshev weight,
not uniform interval measure. Their discussion includes general symmetric
Gegenbauer measures and points to Sawa–Uchida2020, Theorem5.5, for earlier
weighted rational-design existence. I did not locate our uniform R5=20
or degree7 70-node rule here; this is not evidence of historical novelty.
The article distinguishes distinct-node designs from repeated-node
weighted designs, so terminology must be compared carefully.

2026-09-30 17:40 UTC: exact alternate terminology found.
Wen Chean Teh, "Parikh-friendly permutations and uniformly Parikh-friendly
words", Australasian Journal of Combinatorics 76(1) (2020), 208--219.
Primary full text: https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/76/ajc_v76_p208.pdf
Downloaded as teh_uniformly_friendly.pdf; full text read.
Definition 3.5 and Theorem 3.7 identify uniformly Parikh-friendly words
EXACTLY with equal-composition permutation-fair words. Theorem 4.6 constructs
n(n!)^(n-1)-letter words, by all-permutation concatenation increasing deck
symmetry one degree at a time; it actually preserves repeated-letter deck
symmetry, a stronger property than fairness. Section 6 explicitly mentions
using subgroups such as A_n; our representation theorem adds the precise
universal reduced-Lie characterization, not the subgroup suggestion itself.
The construction repeats the older concatenation idea, independently in
word combinatorics terminology. Its mere existence is not a new result here.

Jamie Simpson, "Short witnesses for Parikh-friendly permutations", AJC
78(2) (2020), 329--334, https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/78/ajc_v78_p329.pdf,
proves length <=2n for a witness to ONE alphabet permutation under ONE
ordered Parikh matrix. This is much weaker than uniform Parikh-friendliness
and does not give an exponential/factorial fair-word upper bound.
Search of the exact phrase also found 2024/2025 conference abstracts about
generalized uniformly friendly words and error-correcting applications;
no improved asymptotic construction found in the inspected results. This
is a limited literature search, not a claim that none exists.
