For the 5/4 upgrade I additionally read the entire weighted-success-subset
lemma. The pairwise inclusion difference gives the displayed nonnegative
bias with the correct factor ell/(mN). The ratio bound is valid because
the denominator contains p_i e_(ell-1)(p excluding i,j). The identity
sum_(i<j)(p_i-p_j)^2 = m sum_i(p_i-mean p)^2 supplies the stated bound.
The pairwise covariance formula has numerator E_0 E_2-E_1^2 <= 0 by
Newton's inequality. Its use in the variance is valid because the sampled
coordinates ell(1-p_i) are nonnegative. The variance-plus-squared-bias
bound therefore has exactly the two displayed scales.

On s <= m/256 the stronger finite-odds theorem gives p_i >= 1/2, including
the boundary m=256. Multiplying weighted Taylor error by the average
success weight and applying the weighted moment estimates gives
O(B_0^2 d^2/m). On the complementary rows the former Cauchy estimate has
an exponential margin, so all polynomial factors are absorbed. Extending
the polarization estimate to every row via max u_i <= 256(s+1)/m is valid
and removes the earlier unnecessary truncation there.

The final taper has coefficient sum <= d, so its comparison error is
O(d^4/m+d^2 exp(-cm)). Taking d=floor(kappa m^(1/4)), with an absolute
sufficiently small kappa followed by sufficiently large m, preserves the
strict positive test integral. The reproducing kernel has bounded
coefficient sum and endpoint value one; its error d^2/m is smaller than
1/d. Thus the terminal tilted atom is O(1/d), while the original atom is
smaller by a factor comparable to m. Reflection/complementation preserves
all mixed moments. The stated universal Omega(n^(5/4)) face bound follows.

Scope: this uses the paper's tie-free independent finite-dice model.
Nothing in the proof establishes a sharp exponent, an explicit useful
constant, or an improved bound on total/equal face counts.

SECOND UPGRADE, exponent 3/2: full updated six-page proof reviewed and PASS.

I also checked the sharper weighted-subset corollary and singular Taylor
