Quadratic exceptional-die theorem: shortest complete proof guide
30 September 2026

STATEMENT
Let g(n) be the fewest faces one specified die can have in a
permutation-fair family of n finite uniform dice. Then g(n)=Theta(n^2).
More precisely every die has at least (n-1)^2/200 faces, and every
integer K>=Cn^2 can occur as the count of a specified die. The other
n-1 dice in the upper construction are finite and can all have the
same count, which may be extremely large. This does not solve minimum
total size or polynomial equalization.

NEW SHORT LOWER PROOF: NO ANALYTIC ENDPOINT CHAIN NEEDED
The elementary_quadratic_atom_bound note is self-contained apart from
standard elementary Legendre polynomial identities, with its needed
Gaussian and Radau quadrature facts proved inside the note.

1. A monotone comparison tensor satisfies
     E product_{i in S} p_i = 1/(|S|+1).
   Thus the expectation of every multi-affine polynomial equals its
   diagonal integral over[0,1]. For two columns, stop their completed
   monotone path when either first reaches a prescribed threshold t.
   The two coordinate values are (t,b), up to order, with b<=t. The
   paired difference product is nonnegative on every original row.
   Added path points have zero weight and impose no extra assumptions.

2. Fix a fresh column i. Pair the other columns at all but the largest
   node of a Gaussian rule. Multiply the paired products by a_i-p_i,
   where a_i is the last value of column i. The polynomial is
   nonnegative on rows, but Gaussian exactness leaves just one term
   proportional to a_i minus the largest Gaussian node. Consequently
     a_i >= 1-pi^2/(m+1)^2,  m=n-1,
   for EVERY column. A Jacobi-matrix Rayleigh comparison proves the
   required Gaussian node bound directly.

3. Choose e=floor(m/10) disjoint column pairs at the interior nodes of the
   right Radau rule. Its endpoint weight is1/(e+1)^2 and the sum of
   inverse distances of its interior nodes from1 is e(e+2)/2. These
   identities follow from the Legendre reproducing kernel. The last
   row evaluates the nonnegative paired product at more than half
   its all-one value. Radau exactness therefore gives
     w_last <= 200/m^2.
   The same holds for the total weight of identical terminal rows.
   For a uniform distinguished die w_last=1/K, proving the lower bound.

Only the mixed moment conditions are needed: the distinguished die
need only win every subset containing it with reciprocal-size
probability. Full uniformity of all orders is not required for this
lower-bound argument.

UPPER PROOF
  size_bounds/polynomial_exceptional_die.tex
  size_bounds/centered_bump_simplification.tex (optional variant)
  rational_designs/asymptotic/every_large_exceptional_size.tex
Start with a real equal-weight degree-O(n) interval quadrature with
K nodes, for every integer K>=Cn^2. The published existence input is
Gilboa-Peled Theorem1.1, https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.01505 .
Regularize the nodes, approximate them rationally, and repair all order
integrals with disjoint rational density perturbations of the other
dice. Full zero moments kill mixed correction terms. Rational
constant-density intervals are replaced by finite fair blocks on the
other n-1 labels. The specified count K stays unchanged.
