binomial basis. The valuation inequality j>=(p-1)v_p(j+1) has equality only
at j=0,p-1, so the inverse Gregory series has the claimed unit coefficients

one unit node, so the final count is a positive ordinary integer.

The regularization lemma correctly starts with degree 2m+2. A quadrature
with at most m+1 distinct support points would annihilate the square of
its vanishing polynomial, contradicting its positive integral. Therefore
there are at least m+2 support points and at least m interior ones. The
chosen pivot Jacobian has columns (s*u_q^(s-1))_{s=1}^m and is a scaled
Vandermonde matrix, hence invertible.

For a repeated free coordinate coinciding with a pivot, changing that free
coordinate alone forces the corresponding pivot derivative to -1 and
all other pivot derivatives to zero. Thus the collision equation has
derivative -2 and is not identically zero. A sufficiently small open set
of free-coordinate perturbations moves endpoints inward. A finite union
of proper analytic zero sets cannot cover this open set. Other collisions
are already absent and stay absent after shrinking the neighborhood.
This justifies interior, distinct, equal-weight nodes with K=O(m^2).

The rational correction systems are invertible near the chosen distinct
real nodes, and their right sides vanish at the real design. Therefore all
corrections tend to zero, allowing strict monotonicity to be preserved.
At each index at most one correction is nonzero, so all higher correction
products vanish exactly. The moment equation for an s-element subset is
mu_s+s*a_s=1/(s+1), with no hidden averaging over subsets.
Expanding the complementary factors gives the full local comparison law.
Rational increasing CDF values can indeed be realized by uniform finite
dice after clearing their individual denominators and placing faces in the
specified gaps. This proves only the distinguished-die comparisons;
it does not assert the full order law of the other dice.
