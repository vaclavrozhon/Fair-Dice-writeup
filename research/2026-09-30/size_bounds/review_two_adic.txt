Independent mathematical review, 30 September 2026
Reviewer: size_bounds research agent
Target: ../rational_designs/two_adic_lower_bound.tex

Verdict: I found no gap in the proposed proof that K >= 2^floor(r/2).
The original D/E-series proof and the subsequently proposed shorter
composition proof both pass the checks below. This is a mathematical review,
not a claim that finite computational tests establish the theorem or novelty.

1. Gregory coefficient valuation.
   D(t)=sum 2^j/(j+1) t^j has integral 2-adic coefficients, with reduction
   1+t modulo 2. Thus D^{-1} modulo 2 has every coefficient equal to one.
   Since G(-2t)=D^{-1}, the integral of binom(x,j) has valuation exactly -j.
   This is a formal-power-series argument and requires no evaluation at t=1.

2. Integer-valued binomial polynomials.
   For rational y in Z_2, binom(y,j) lies in Z_2, by continuity from the
   nonnegative integers. Therefore exactness for binom(x,r) indeed forces
   v_2(K)>=r when all nodes are 2-adically integral.

3. Parity polynomial.
   H_r(y)=sum_{j=1}^r (-2)^{j-1} binom(y,j) agrees with the odd/even
   indicator modulo 2^r on nonnegative integers. The omitted terms start
   with a coefficient divisible by 2^r. H_r and the parity indicator are
   continuous on Z_2, so the congruence extends to all Z_2. No ordering or
   Archimedean bound on y is used.

4. Original coefficient-tail proof.
   The coefficients of D have valuations j-v_2(j+1) tending to infinity,
   so the series and its derivative converge on Z_2. The 2-adic logarithm
   series is valid at -1=1-2; log(-1)=0, hence D(1)=0. Therefore the
   coefficients of E=D/(1-t) are tails of the convergent D series.
   The bound j-v_2(j+1)>=ceil((j-1)/2) is valid (including j=1 and j=3,
   where the coarse bound is attained). It gives v_2(e_k)>=ceil(k/2).
   Convolution and recursive formal inversion preserve this coefficient
   class. These series, and their derivatives, converge at 1.
   Differentiating D gives D'(1)=-1, so E(1)=1; evaluating E^{-1} is
   legitimate and gives F_2(-2)=1. The doubling factor for F_{2M} has
   the required valuations, including the exceptional linear term -M.
   Its value at t=1 is one because M is even. The final tail division by
   two gives ceil((r+1)/2)-1=floor(r/2), with no off-by-one loss.

5. Shorter composition proof communicated by the author.
   The formal identity F_N(z)=G((1+z)^N-1) is correct because
   log((1+z)^N)=N log(1+z) formally.
   For even N, W(t)=(1-2t)^N-1 has no constant term, and its degree-k
   coefficient has valuation at least ceil(k/2)+1: check k=1 separately
   using v_2(N)>=1, while for k>=2 use k>=ceil(k/2)+1.
   Since v_2(G_j)=-j, each degree-k contribution to G_j W^j has valuation
   at least ceil(k/2). Moreover every coefficient of W has valuation at
   least 2, so each such contribution has valuation at least j.
   Consequently the double family can be rearranged and evaluated on Z_2:
   modulo 2^L only j<L and k<2L can contribute. This makes the formal
   coefficient calculation compatible with analytic evaluation at t=1.
   At that point W(1)=0, so the full series evaluates to G(0)=1. The tail
   estimate is then exactly the same as in the original proof.

6. Final divisibility argument.
   Minimal a with 2^a*x_i in Z_2 exists for a finite rational list and
   ensures at least one odd normalized node. Its number q is a positive
   ordinary integer, at most K. The congruence gives
   v_2(q)>=min(r,v_2(K)+floor(r/2)), hence q>=2^floor(r/2).
   Positivity here concerns the count q, not the signs or locations of
   the rational quadrature nodes. Repeated nodes cause no issue.

7. Mild stronger consequence already implicit in the theorem.
   If K<2^r, then necessarily K/2^v_2(K)>=2^floor(r/2).
   Indeed, if v_2(K)+floor(r/2)>=r the refined q divisibility contradicts
   q<=K<2^r. Otherwise divide K>=q>=2^(v_2(K)+floor(r/2)) by 2^v_2(K).
   In particular, if K is a power of two then K>=2^r.

8. Independent exact checks.
   independent_checks.py constructs binomial polynomials directly using
   Fraction arithmetic, integrates coefficients, and does not use D/E or
   the composition identity. It checked the Gregory valuations through
   degree 70, the scaled-integral bounds for a=1,...,6 through degree 70,
   and the parity congruence at 3500 rational inputs/degrees. All passed.
   Results are in independent_checks.json. The same script independently
   verifies the Jacobi identities needed for the insertion barrier.

Scope restriction: the theorem does not prove an exponential bound for
each die. A general local comparison tensor has different rational CDF
coordinates for different other dice. The one-variable rational-design
construction only provides a sufficient mechanism for constructing fair
dice, not a necessary representation of every die.
